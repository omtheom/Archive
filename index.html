Frequently Asked Questions

Questions families ask us.

Why should I preserve these things now?

Because family archives are often scattered across houses, offices, computers, phones and storage devices — and the people who know what they contain may be the only people who can explain their significance.

Preserving them while that knowledge is still available makes it possible to organise the collection properly and give future generations a record they can understand.

You don't have to wait until something is lost to realise that it was irreplaceable.

Is this only for wealthy families or large collections?

No. The value of an archive isn't determined by how much it is worth financially. A box of photographs, a collection of letters or a handwritten diary may have no commercial value and still be irreplaceable to a family.

Our service is designed to help families preserve the material that matters to them, whether that collection is small or extensive.

Is this a one-time payment?

Yes. The Legacy Archive is designed as a one-time preservation service. You pay for the preservation work and the agreed preservation capacity once. There are no annual storage fees or recurring subscription payments for the preservation service.

If you later decide to preserve additional material, that would be treated as a new preservation project.

What exactly am I paying for?

You are paying for more than storage.

Your fee covers the professional work required to prepare your material for long-term preservation, including assessment, organisation, digitisation where required, archival preparation, preservation arrangements and your preservation documentation.

The exact scope depends on the material you want preserved.

What kinds of things can I preserve?

You can preserve many different types of family material, including:

  • Photographs
  • Letters and correspondence
  • Diaries and journals
  • Certificates and important documents
  • Books and manuscripts
  • Family videos
  • Audio recordings
  • Interviews and oral histories
  • Newspaper articles and clippings
  • Family business records
  • Historical photographs
  • Other important digital files

If you're unsure whether something can be preserved, tell us about it and we'll assess it with you.

Do I have to digitise everything myself?

No. If your material is physical, such as photographs, letters, documents or manuscripts, we can discuss the digitisation requirements with you and arrange the appropriate preparation.

You don't need to understand archival technology. That's our job.

What if my material is already digital?

That's even simpler. You can provide your existing digital material to us and we will assess, organise and prepare it for preservation. This could include material currently stored on:

  • Computers
  • External hard drives
  • USB drives
  • Cloud storage
  • CDs or DVDs
  • Other digital storage
Do I need to know how much data I have?

No. If you don't know whether your collection is 20 GB, 80 GB or 500 GB, don't worry.

Tell us what you have and we'll help determine the approximate size and the appropriate preservation option.

What happens if I have more than 100 GB?

We offer a standard family package for archives of up to 100 GB.

If your collection is larger, we'll assess it and recommend an appropriate preservation configuration and provide you with a quotation. You don't need to split your archive yourself.

Can I preserve an entire family history?

Yes. In fact, that is one of the purposes of the service.

A family archive can bring together photographs, documents, letters, books, recordings, videos and other materials from different generations into one organised collection.

Can I preserve my family's business records?

Yes. We can work with family businesses and established companies to preserve historical documents, photographs, correspondence, records and other material that forms part of the company's history.

For substantial business archives, we provide a separate assessment and quotation.

Can a company or organisation use this service?

Yes. We also work with organisations that have significant historical or institutional archives.

This can include family businesses, foundations, clubs, associations, schools, religious organisations and other established institutions. Larger institutional archives are assessed individually.

Where will my archive be preserved?

We arrange long-term preservation through specialist archival infrastructure appropriate to the preservation project.

The exact preservation arrangement will be explained to you as part of your project.

How long will my archive be preserved?

The service is designed for long-term preservation, rather than ordinary cloud storage or a conventional backup.

The preservation medium and facility are selected specifically for long-term archival purposes. We will provide you with the relevant preservation documentation for your archive.

Is this the same as backing up my files to Google Drive or an external hard drive?

No. A backup is primarily intended to help you recover files if your computer or storage device fails. Archival preservation has a different purpose: to prepare important information for long-term survival and future access.

We recommend that families continue to maintain ordinary working copies of their important files as well.

Will I still have access to my files?

Yes. Your preservation archive is not intended to replace the working copies you use in everyday life.

We recommend keeping accessible copies of your important material separately while the preservation archive serves as a long-term record. The exact access arrangements depend on the preservation configuration selected for your archive.

What happens to my original photographs and documents?

We don't want you to part with irreplaceable originals unnecessarily.

Where physical materials are being digitised, we'll discuss the handling, collection, digitisation and return arrangements with you before work begins. Your originals remain important family property.

Will you keep my information confidential?

Yes. Family archives can contain extremely private material.

We treat the materials entrusted to us as confidential and use them only for the purposes agreed with you. Our preservation agreement will set out the relevant confidentiality, ownership and handling terms.

Who owns my archive?

You do.

The preservation of your material does not transfer ownership of your photographs, documents, manuscripts, recordings or other intellectual property to Mapel Firm. Ownership and permitted use will be clearly addressed in the preservation agreement.

Can you preserve sensitive or private family material?

Yes, subject to the nature of the material and the applicable legal and technical requirements.

During your consultation, we'll discuss what you want preserved and any special confidentiality or access requirements.

Will I receive proof that my archive was preserved?

Yes. At the completion of your project, you receive documentation recording the preservation work, together with a Mapel Certificate of Preservation.

The certificate identifies the archive and records the preservation project.

Can I add more material later?

Yes, but additional material is treated as a separate preservation project.

Your original preservation payment covers the archive and capacity agreed for that project. If you later discover another collection of photographs, documents or recordings that you want preserved, we can assess the additional material and provide a new quotation.

Do I have to preserve everything I own?

No. You can preserve a carefully selected collection. You might choose to preserve:

  • Your parents' photographs
  • Your grandfather's correspondence
  • Your family's business history
  • Your mother's journals
  • Important family documents
  • A lifetime of photographs
  • A particular person's work

The archive should contain the things your family believes are worth carrying forward.
